House First, Then the Business

Save for a house, buy it before 35, save again, then decide about the cafe. Four steps in that order — and only the first two have to be settled now.

I Preface

Nothing here is urgent. The value is in the order of the steps, and in knowing exactly where the one fixed date sits.

Each step carries its own short list of actions, so that what to do sits beside the reasoning for doing it. Ticks are remembered in this browser alone, so the authoritative list should live somewhere you trust.

Figures in the text carry a superscript pointing to the source that supports them↓, official or primary wherever one exists. Where a figure is arithmetic performed here rather than quoted, it is marked calc.

Prices, interest rates and market averages are deliberately absent. They move every quarter, and including them would make this document wrong within months. What remains are the rules that determine outcomes: how much a salary will support, which costs cannot be financed, and which entitlements expire at 35.

II The position

Where things stand at the outset, and what that supports.

Observation

Income is not the binding constraint

At approximately €80,000, the conventional multiple supports a first purchase comfortably. What constrains the plan is smaller and more tractable: several thousand euros of cash that no lender will advance, and a permanent contract. Both are within reach in the time available.

The multiple is a rule of thumb rather than a rule. The actual maximum comes from tables revised each year and moves with the interest rate and with any existing debts, which is why the figure worth acting on is the one an adviser puts in writing.6

Observation

The margin created by May 2028

A May 2028 target sits roughly three years ahead of the age-35 threshold, and that margin changes the character of the deadline. It functions as a fallback rather than a constraint.

A contract that converts late, a decision to save for longer, or a purchase that collapses shortly before completion would each cost time without ending the plan. The date can move more than once and still leave the entitlement intact.

III The four steps

Sequential, and each one funds the next.

Step one

Save for the house

Now to May 2028 · approximately 21 months

A house bought to live in can be financed to 100% of its assessed value. The mortgage covers the purchase price in full. What it does not cover are the costs that sit on top — notary, valuation, mortgage advice — and these must be met from your own funds.4

Step one therefore reduces to a comparatively modest sum: €6,000 to €10,000. Spread across 21 months, reaching €10,000 requires roughly €480 a month; reaching €15,000, which would also cover a buying agent and leave a margin, requires about €715.calc

The other half of this step costs nothing at all: settling the employment contract. Lenders assess a permanent contract quite differently from a temporary one, and where conversion has not yet happened, an intentieverklaring — a written statement from the employer of their intention to make the position permanent — is generally accepted in its place.6

Two further matters affect borrowing capacity. Student debt is assessed on the current monthly repayment rather than the original balance.6 And no Dutch property should be acquired in any form before this one, since the first-time-buyer exemption may be claimed only once and any earlier use exhausts it. Property held abroad is irrelevant to this: the test is whether the exemption has been claimed before, not whether a home has ever been owned.2,3

Actions

Step two

Buy the first house, before 35

Target May 2028 · final date: the 35th birthday

A purchase made under 35, to live in, by someone who has not claimed the exemption before attracts no transfer tax at all rather than 2%. A value ceiling applies, but it sits far above the relevant range. The exemption is claimed at the notary on the day of transfer, which means it is the transfer date that must precede the birthday, not the date the contract is signed.1,2

The loan will also fall well below the ceiling for NHG, the national mortgage guarantee. It costs a modest one-off premium and returns the lowest available interest rate together with genuine protection should the house have to be sold after job loss, illness or divorce.5

The stepping stone as a deliberate choice

Treating the first house as temporary is worth stating plainly, because it changes the standard it has to meet. The question is not whether this is the house to keep. The question is whether it is worth owning during the years the exemption is still available.

Three consequences follow.

  • Claiming the exemption now is the only way to claim it. It lapses at 35 regardless. Reserving it for a later, better house is not an option, because there is no later point at which it still exists. The second purchase will attract 2%, and that is simply the cost of moving up.1
  • Selling on is unrestricted. Letting may not be. Many municipalities operate opkoopbescherming, which prohibits letting the property without a permit for four years after transfer. It imposes no obligation to live there and places no restriction on selling; what it prevents is becoming a landlord. The mortgage will carry its own occupancy clause in addition.8,9
  • Moving again incurs the buyer's costs again. Which argues for a house worth living in for several years, rather than the cheapest property that satisfies the deadline.

Caution

Any bid above the valuation is paid in cash

The mortgage covers 100% of the assessed value, not 100% of the agreed price. Where a property is won by bidding above the valuation, the difference is paid from your own funds, in addition to the buyer's costs.4

This is the argument for saving beyond the strict minimum. A target set exactly at the minimum leaves no capacity to compete for any property that attracts a higher offer.

Actions

Step three

Save again, then the cafe

After the house · a slower and longer phase

The economics invert at this point. The house was inexpensive to enter because a mortgage carried almost all of it. A cafe is the reverse: nearly every euro must be your own or borrowed at commercial rates, and the sums are considerably larger.

The least expensive route is to lease premises already fitted for hospitality and take them over as a going concern. Even so, entry costs begin somewhere near €90,000 once the deposit, the takeover, a cosmetic refresh and sufficient working capital for the opening months are accounted for.

Converting a bare unit costs considerably more, because the fit-out is the largest item in the budget — extraction, grease trap, heavier power supply, sanitary facilities, acoustic insulation. Under a lease, most of that expenditure remains with the building when the tenancy ends.12

Observation

Where the largest saving lies

Premises with functioning hospitality installations already in place avoid the single largest cost in the project. A bare unit appears cheaper per square metre and, in practice, almost always proves more expensive.

Lease the premises rather than buying them

Purchasing commercial premises attracts transfer tax at 10.4%, the rate applied to anything that is not a dwelling, and one that remained unchanged while the residential rates were revised. It can be neither financed nor recovered, and banks lend a good deal less against commercial property than against a house.1 Lenders also expect two to three years of trading figures before considering a loan against a building, which will not exist at the outset.11

A lease is not a weak position here. Hospitality premises fall within a protected category of Dutch tenancy law, running in five-year terms with an automatic second term and substantial protection for the tenant. Ten years of security is sufficient to build something durable.14

Caution

Business debt can reach the house

A sole trader and the business are the same legal person, so business debts are personal debts. Even within a company structure, lenders routinely require a personal guarantee from a first-time proprietor — and by then the most valuable asset available to secure it is the house.10

This is not an argument against the business. It is an argument for reading what is signed, and for keeping the distinction clear between borrowing for the cafe and pledging the house against it.

Actions

Step four

The longer term

Once the house is owned and the cafe is trading

Deliberately left open. Reaching this point brings information unavailable now: whether the cafe earns, what the house has become worth, and whether hospitality still appeals.

The plausible options are a larger house, purchasing the premises already leased, a second property, or simply carrying less debt. None can be chosen sensibly today, so nothing is listed here to act on.

Three things will hold true whenever the question arises:

  • A trading record is what opens business lending. Two or three years of figures materially changes what a bank will consider.11
  • Government-backed small-business credit has a ceiling, so beyond a certain scale the ordinary banks become unavoidable.11
  • Buying property to let is a different regime from buying to occupy — less lending, heavier taxation, more regulation — and those rules have been revised repeatedly.1,8

IV Money

Three aspects of the cash that are commonly misunderstood.

What the cash is for

It is not a deposit. For a first house the Dutch system does not really require one, since the mortgage covers the price. What is being saved for is the set of costs that cannot be financed and fall due on the day of transfer.4

The house and the cafe draw on the same savings

They are not separate budgets, which is the substantive reason the order matters. Buying the house first consumes a small amount of cash and leaves an asset behind. Starting the cafe first would consume everything available and leave you still renting.

There is a second reason, of timing. The exemption lapses at 35, and a young business tends to reduce what a lender will offer, because self-employed income is assessed on several years of accounts rather than on a payslip. Beginning with the business therefore makes the mortgage harder to obtain during precisely the years in which the exemption is still available.6

Why letting property is not the opening move

Property bought to let is the weaker proposition at every point of entry. Less can be borrowed against it, transfer tax is 8% rather than nothing, neither the exemption nor NHG is available, the tax treatment is heavier, and rents are capped across most of this price range. It also fails the basic test of housing you, since rent would still be payable elsewhere.1,8

It remains a reasonable option at step four, once a house is owned and equity has accumulated. It is not a way to begin.

V Terms

The Dutch vocabulary, in roughly the order it will be encountered.

Transfer tax: the four rates

A single tax carries four rates, according to what is bought and what is done with it. Confusing them is the most expensive error available in this plan. The 8% rate for dwellings that are not a main residence was reduced from a higher figure in 2026, while the 10.4% rate for everything that is not a dwelling was left unchanged — which is why older commentary found online frequently conflicts.1

Glossary

kosten koper — buyer's costs
The costs above the purchase price: notary, valuation, advice, and transfer tax where due. Always met from your own funds, never from the mortgage. Listings marked k.k. indicate these fall to the buyer.
startersvrijstelling — first-time-buyer exemption
Nil transfer tax in place of 2%. Requires being under 35 at the moment of transfer, occupying the dwelling for the longer term, never having claimed it before, and a value below the ceiling. Declared at the notary.2
NHG — national mortgage guarantee
A state-backed guarantee available below a loan ceiling. A one-off premium secures the lowest interest rate and can cover residual debt where the house must be sold after job loss, illness, divorce or death.5
intentieverklaring — statement of intent
A written statement from an employer of their intention to offer a permanent contract. Lenders accept it in place of one, which makes it the least expensive way to unblock a mortgage application.
opkoopbescherming — buy-up protection
A municipal rule prohibiting the letting of a dwelling without a permit for four years after transfer. Common in cities. It imposes no duty to live there and no restriction on selling.8
Starterslening — starter loan
A supplementary municipal loan alongside the mortgage, carrying no monthly payment in the early years. Amounts differ by municipality, budgets are exhausted, and it generally requires this to be a first owner-occupied home.7
VvE — owners' association
Buying an apartment carries automatic membership. Its finances, reserve fund and rules pass to you, and are therefore part of what is being bought.
Sociale Hygiëne — social hygiene diploma
Required for an alcohol licence, valid for life, and obtainable years before any premises exist.15
casco — bare shell
A unit without fit-out. Cheaper per square metre and usually more expensive in total, since every installation is paid for from scratch.

VI Contingencies

Four departures from the plan, and what each one actually costs.

Open questions

What this document cannot settle

  • The actual maximum loan. Only an adviser produces that, and it depends on existing debts and the particular lender.6
  • Which municipality. It governs price, the availability of a starter loan, and the letting rules simultaneously.9
  • Whether residence status bears on any of this. Nothing here examines it.
  • Whether the purchase is made alone or jointly. Two incomes change the borrowing arithmetic, and both names on the deed carry consequences worth an hour of advice — though the plan holds either way.
